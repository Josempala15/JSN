Why litter matters

Litter doesn’t just stay where it’s dropped. Wind and rain wash it into drains, rivers and eventually the sea, where animals eat it or get tangled up in it. Over 900 different species are known to be affected by rubbish in the sea!

Plastic also breaks down into tiny bits called microplastics that end up in our soil and water. Basically, every bit we pick up helps.

Source: Kühn & van Franeker (2020), Marine Pollution Bulletin – summary.

How long does litter last?

Longer than you’d think! Scientists left cigarette butts outside for 10 years, and on a street about half of each butt was still there at the end – just broken up into tiny bits of plastic.

So a butt someone flicks on the pavement today could still be around in 10 years.

Source: Bonanomi et al. (2026), Environmental Pollution – doi.

What you can do

  • Take your rubbish home if the bin’s full.
  • Carry a reusable bottle and bag.
  • Buy second-hand clothes and fix things instead of binning them.
  • See litter you can’t pick up? Snap a photo and report it on the map.
  • Join or organise a clean on the Community cleans tab !

Litter-pick safely

  • Wear gloves and proper shoes, and use a litter picker if you’ve got one.
  • Never touch needles, broken glass or chemicals – report them to your council instead.
  • Stay away from busy roads, steep banks and fast water.
  • Leave nests, plants and animals alone – only take the rubbish.

Privacy policy

Last updated: October 2026

Who I am

JSN is run by Jo. If you have any questions about your data, or want it changed or deleted, email me at Josn1505@gmail.com.

What I collect

  • Your account: the nickname you choose and an account ID. You never give me your name or email. Your recovery code works as your password and is stored securely by Supabase (it can’t be read back).
  • Reports: the photo, its location on the map, the type of litter, your note and the time. Reports, photos and their locations are public – anyone can see them on the map.
  • Community cleans: cleans you create, cleans you RSVP to and kit you request. Your nickname is shown to other people on these.
  • Good news: anything you post, shown publicly with your nickname.
  • Emails: if you email me (for example about kit), I’ll see your email address and only use it to reply to you.

Photos and location

The location is read from your photo on your own device. Before uploading, the photo is shrunk and saved again, which removes its hidden details (like the camera and the exact time it was taken). Please don’t photograph people’s faces, car number plates or the inside of anyone’s home.

Stored on your device

Your browser keeps your recovery code (so you stay signed in), which notifications you’ve seen and the button cooldowns. There are no advertising or tracking cookies.

Who else handles data

  • Supabase stores the database and photos.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the website.
  • OpenStreetMap provides the street map and the address search, so your searches and map views are sent to them.
  • jsDelivr / unpkg deliver some of the code the site runs on.

These services may see your IP address when your browser connects to them. I never sell or share your data with anyone else.

Why I use it

Only to run JSN – showing litter on the map, organising cleans and sharing good news. The legal basis is legitimate interests (running the site you’ve chosen to use).

How long I keep it

Until you delete it or ask me to. You can delete your own reports, kit requests and good news posts yourself. To delete your whole account, email me.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete your data, or object to how it’s used – just email me. If you’re unhappy with how I’ve handled it, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Under 13?

Please ask a parent or carer before making an account, and always go to cleans with an adult you trust.

Changes

If this policy changes, I’ll update it here and change the date at the top.

Questions?

If you have any questions feel free to email me at Josn1505@gmail.com – Jo
